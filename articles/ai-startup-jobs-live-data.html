Why Daily Diffs Beat Static Job Indexes in AI

Job data reaches buyers three ways—indexed databases, page scrapers, and live ATS pulls—and the gap between them is widest wherever hiring shifts quickest. An indexed database is a snapshot: it still displays postings that closed yesterday while missing roles that went live this morning because its crawler has not returned yet. A page scraper fetches HTML at run time, only to break whenever a company redesigns its careers site. A live ATS pull queries the exact feed powering the company's own careers page. When an engineering or research team closes a role, it vanishes from the feed—and from your dataset—immediately. We walk through the underlying API mechanics in our guide to live job data from ATS APIs; in AI, where market participants react to every hiring signal, stale listings carry real cost.

Because every pull reflects the employer's live board at run time, the practical unit of value is the diff between consecutive runs. Run the same pack tomorrow and compare the two outputs: newly appearing records are openings, vanished records are closures, and both represent genuine hiring events rather than crawl artefacts. A dataset built this way never hands you a job you cannot apply for, because closed postings drop out the day they close.

That clean daily diff turns raw postings into three market readings without requiring any external data source:

  • Role-family shifts: Job titles map the industry's evolving structure—applied research scientists and ML engineers at frontier labs, forward-deployed engineers at applied-AI companies, GPU and infrastructure specialists at the platform layer, and newer evaluations and safety roles that did not exist as a category two years ago. Tracking which families expand fastest quarter by quarter provides a leading indicator of capital allocation before funding announcements appear.
  • Geographic concentration: Structured location fields—city, region, country, and workplace type—show whether AI hiring is clustering in hubs like San Francisco or dispersing into remote-first teams, letting talent buyers answer the question from primary data rather than headlines.
  • Hiring velocity: Comparing daily runs measures expansion and contraction by company and sector. A lab adding twenty roles in a week is scaling an initiative; a startup whose board shrinks for a month is consolidating or slowing down. Where a week-old index turns a hiring surge into history, a live pull surfaces it while it is still actionable.

How We Built and Curated the Pack

A curated dataset is defined as much by what it rejects as by what it ships. Keeping every live board that happens to match a well-known brand name inflates headcount while destroying signal. When we probed 307 candidate boards for the AI pack, we dropped 4 that returned live jobs because keeping them would have made the pack dishonest. First, the Greenhouse board xai turned out to be xAI's annotation-staffing pool: 300 near-identical temporary "AI Tutor" contract roles listed under the entity name SpaceXSI rather than the company's core hiring. Second, the board andurilindustries held 2,600 jobs—a fifth of all probed volume—of general defense-manufacturing hiring; Anduril is not a startup and would have swallowed the pack's identity, so we dropped it too, alongside a defense RF/hardware board (epirus) and an ambiguous pylon board that could not be verified as the support platform of the same name. We also verified the zeroes honestly: 48 Lever and 9 SmartRecruiters candidates returned no live AI-startup postings, and we sanity-checked the Lever endpoint against the known-good binance board (HTTP 200, real postings) before trusting that the zeroes were real rather than a probe bug.

Had we left those four exclusions in, the pack would carry 13,000 jobs of noise. Instead, the 128 boards that ship represent the verified AI cohort, checked individually against live postings with identity checks on sample job content. We document the same verification discipline—handling slug-verification traps, name collisions, and misleading boards—in our guide on live Ashby job data.

Curated lists also decay over time as startups switch ATS providers or retire slugs. To prevent silent drift, we re-verify the entire pack at every version build, and any board that fails during a run is reported explicitly in the run summary rather than dropped without notice. You are also not restricted to the default 128 companies: pass your own boards in the companies input across all five supported systems (Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, SmartRecruiters, and Workable). When monitoring custom lists, a previously active company flatlining to zero in the run summary is the clearest signature of an ATS migration.
