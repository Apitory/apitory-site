Live Ashby Job Data: Tracking Hiring at the Companies Building AI

Ashby is the applicant tracking system of the AI industry. Its public posting feeds give you live, structured job data from the companies that matter most to anyone watching AI hiring — if you know where the feeds are and what they do and do not carry.

Why Ashby, why now

Every hiring market has a centre of gravity: the system where the employers that define the market actually run their hiring. For technology, and especially for AI, that system is increasingly Ashby. OpenAI, Cohere, Perplexity, Cursor, Harvey, Ramp, Notion, Linear — the companies whose hiring decisions are read as industry signals — run their career boards on Ashby. A job-data buyer who only watches the generalist boards sees these companies late, through an index, and misses the texture. A buyer who pulls Ashby's feeds directly sees the same listings the companies' own careers pages display, at the moment they display them.

Our Ashby Jobs — Live API Actor is built on exactly that: it queries Ashby's official public posting API for a list of company boards and returns every open role, normalised into one schema, at $3.00 per 1,000 job results. It ships with a default pack of 140 Ashby boards, verified live at build time with 6,961 open jobs counted across them — a pack assembled by probing 639 candidate slugs against the live API and keeping only boards that actually return postings. The pack is deliberately heavy on AI labs, AI infrastructure, GPU cloud and dev tools, with fintech, health-AI, security and SaaS around them, because that is where Ashby's centre of gravity sits.

What an Ashby record contains — and what it honestly does not

Because the data comes from the system the employer maintains, the fields are the employer's own: title, department, team, employment type, workplace type (Remote / Hybrid / OnSite), structured location with city, region and country broken out, posting date, application URL, and the full description text — always included, with no second pass needed. Ashby's structured location data is a real advantage over Greenhouse, whose boards do not carry the same breakdown; if your use case filters by geography, Ashby records classify cleanly where other systems force you to parse free text.

Two honest gaps belong in the same paragraph, because they shape how the data should be used. Ashby's public feed carries no company display name: the record's company field is always null, and the board slug in companyId is the identifier — openai, ramp, linear. And it carries no language or experience-level field, so those come back null rather than guessed from the description text. We return what the source actually carries. The slug-as-identity is a small adjustment for buyers (your company list is a slug list), and it is also the reason the pack's curation mattered: every slug was verified against real posting content, because a slug alone tells you nothing about which company it belongs to. Our build caught the traps — a Dutch grocery company sharing a name with a chat platform, a cyber-defense firm sharing a name with a CRM — and the pack that ships is the verified one.

Live pull vs. index: the freshness argument, Ashby edition

Job data reaches buyers three ways — indexed databases, page scrapers, live ATS pulls — and the differences are sharpest exactly where hiring moves fastest, which is AI. An index is a snapshot: it shows you postings that closed yesterday and misses the ones that opened this morning, because its crawler has not visited yet. A scraper reads career pages at run time but breaks every time a company redesigns its site, and it can only see the companies someone built a parser for. A live pull asks the ATS for a company's current openings and returns exactly what the company's own careers page is showing, because it is the same feed. When a role closes, it disappears from the feed — and from your results — immediately.

The practical consequence is the diff. Run the same board list tomorrow and compare the two datasets: new records are openings, vanished records are closures, and both are real events rather than crawl artefacts. For the use cases that dominate Ashby data — AI job boards, competitor hiring monitoring, talent-market research — the diff is the product. A board that never shows a closed role is a board applicants trust; a watchlist that surfaces a competitor's new AI-research opening the day it appears is a watchlist worth paying for. Who buys Ashby job data, and for what

AI job boards and newsletters. A board for AI roles does not need the whole web; it needs complete, current coverage of the companies that define AI hiring. The 140-board pack is that coverage, pre-verified, and the diff between daily runs is the board's freshest content — "new roles this week" is generated, not written.

Recruiting and talent intelligence. Department, team and location fields show where a rival is investing before it shows up in a press release. A hiring spike in a competitor's research team is a strategy signal; the same spike at a client is a sales signal — the company is hiring, which means it has budget and a problem.

Market research. Live postings with full text support skills analysis, remote-policy tracking and hiring-velocity measurement across the AI sector, on a time series you can trust because the timestamps come from the employer's own system.

Lead generation. Companies hiring aggressively are companies buying tools. Filtered by department, live openings are a buying-signal feed for anyone selling into that function — engineering tools to engineering hiring, sales tools to sales hiring.

The pack, the cap, and keeping the list alive

Three operational facts complete the picture. First, the pack is ordered by open-job count ascending, so a capped default run samples broadly across companies before reaching the mega-boards (OpenAI at 876 open roles would otherwise swallow the whole run). The default cap is 500 records; raise it or trim the list to exhaust specific boards. Second, the pack decays: companies switch ATS and slugs die, so the pack is re-verified at every version build, and boards that fail are reported in the run summary rather than silently dropped. Third, the Actor is Ashby-only by design — if a company on your list runs Greenhouse, it belongs to the sibling Greenhouse Jobs — Live API, or to the multi-system parent Career Site Jobs — Live ATS API, which covers all five platforms. Use the cut that matches your company list; the engine underneath is the same.

At $0.003 per job record, the arithmetic is small and proportional: a watchlist of 140 boards returning roughly 500 jobs per daily pull costs about $1.50 a day — and a quiet day, when your companies have few openings, costs proportionally less, because you pay for records delivered, not for companies checked. There is no subscription, no monthly minimum, and the run summary (which boards were pulled, what failed) goes to the key-value store, never into your dataset — you are never charged for a non-job record. Ashby vs. Greenhouse: which cut fits your list

Buyers with mixed company lists often ask whether to use the Ashby-only Actor, the Greenhouse-only sibling, or the multi-system parent — and the answer is genuinely a property of the list, not of the tools. The three share one engine and one price ($3.00 per 1,000 jobs), so the decision is about coverage and field shape. Ashby records carry structured city, region and country, team, employment type and workplace as first-class fields, and full description text always; Greenhouse records carry the company display name, which Ashby lacks, and their own structured quirks. A list that lives entirely on one platform should use that platform's Actor — the input is smaller, the defaults are tuned to it, and the run summary reads in that platform's terms. A list that spans platforms belongs to the multi-system Career Site Jobs — Live ATS API, which normalises all five into one schema and lets you watch a company through an ATS migration without changing tools. And a buyer building an AI-focused product should know the market's own answer: the AI cohort has consolidated onto Ashby and Greenhouse — our B3 pack build found no AI-startup boards with live postings on Lever or SmartRecruiters — so the two single-system Actors plus the parent cover the AI hiring universe in practice, and the choice between them is, in the end, a one-line question: which systems do your companies run on?

FAQ

How fresh is the data?

The Actor queries Ashby's public posting API at run time. Results reflect what each company's own careers page displays at that moment, because the careers page is served from the same system. There is no Apitory-side index between you and the source.

Which companies are in the pack?

140 boards verified live at build time, holding 6,961 open jobs: AI labs and model companies, AI infrastructure and dev platforms, GPU cloud, fintech, SaaS, data and analytics, health-AI, security and recruiting tech. The full sector breakdown is in the Actor's README.

Why is the company field null?

Ashby's public feed does not carry a company display name. The board slug in companyId identifies the company. Slugs in the preloaded pack were verified against real posting content at build time.

Can I pull companies outside the pack?

Yes — pass any Ashby board slugs in the companies input. The pack is the default, not the limit.

What does it cost to monitor 140 boards daily?

$3.00 per 1,000 job records delivered. A 500-job daily pull costs about $1.50 in Actor charges. Boards with no open roles cost nothing beyond the run's start event.

What if a company leaves Ashby?

Its board stops returning postings and the failure is reported in the run summary, not silently dropped. The pack is re-verified at every version build; the same pattern applies to your own lists — a previously active company flatlining to zero is the signature of a migration.
